One number, Zn(β) = Tr e−βH, read two ways. Left: a classical toy where both readings are exact. Right: a quantum ring diagonalised in this page (Jacobi, all 2n or 3n states).
1Physical reading: Gibbs weights over energy levels. Their purity T(n,β) = Zn(2β)/Zn(β)2. Large β drives it to 1.
2Spatial reading: Zn = Σ λin, the transfer spectrum across one bond. Purity S(n,β) = Z2n(β)/Zn(β)2; large n drives it to 1. The paper's kernel is real symmetric, so even powers are weights.
3maxk pk ≥ Σ pk2. So T > 1/2 forces a unique ground state, and e−βγ ≤ u/(1 − u), u = 1 − T. Turn β up on the quantum ring: the bound closes on the true gap.

Four partition functions sit at the corners of a 2×2 square in the (n, β) plane. Each purity is a ratio of two of them. Slide the square with the knobs.
1Write S(n,2β) T(n,β)2 and S(n,β)2 T(2n,β) as Z's: upstairs and downstairs.
2Every Z appears once upstairs and once downstairs. The pairs cancel, and both sides are Z2n(2β) / Zn(β)4. Trivial algebra, not previously exploited.
3S(n,2β) = S(n,β)2 T(2n,β) / T(n,β)2: movement along β is paid for with movement along n. Slide the square: it holds to every shown digit.

Purity Σ pk2 is close to 1 exactly when one entry dominates. The defect is u = 1 − Σ pk2. Shape the vector.
1Square and renormalise: the dominant entry pulls away quadratically. Lemma 4.1: the new defect is at most u2/(2(1 − u)2), about u2/2.
2Gibbs weights squared are Gibbs weights at 2β, because e−2βE = (e−βE)2. Squaring moves T along the β axis.
3Spatial weights squared are spatial weights at 2n, because |λ|2n = (|λ|n)2. Squaring moves S along the n axis.
4Neither move alone reaches (2n, 2β). The two identities trade the axes: a bound on T at larger n is paid for with bounds on S, and back.

Suppose both defects are at most u0 at one (n, β). The paper certifies u0 = 1/8 at (60, 105/4). Shown: u0 = 0.1, C = 5, the explainer's toy run.
1One turn: along β, S pays a factor T2 (defect 3u) and squares to (3u)2/2; along n, T pays S2 (defect 2u) and squares to 2u2. Together u1 ≤ C u02, C < 7.9 for u ≤ 1/8.
2Iterate: u, Cu2, C3u4, C7u8. Doubly exponential in the number of doublings, while nj = 2jn0 and βj = 2jβ0 only double.
3e−βjγ ≤ 3uj gives γ ≥ log(1/3uj)/βj. Both grow like 2j, so the bound flattens at log(1/r)/β0, r = Cu0. Press P: the paper's r = 79/80 gives 4.8×10−4.
4Why not every chain: T → 1 needs vβ ≫ n, S → 1 needs n ≫ vβ. Gapless: both cannot hold, best defects 0.3 to 0.5, Cu0 > 1, the machine stalls. Gapped: the two demands decouple.

paperTheorem 1.1. HL = Σj Sj·Sj+1, spin 1, periodic. For every even L ≥ 60 the ground state is unique and γL > (4/105) log(80/79) ≈ 4.8×10−4. For every even L ≥ 2304, γL > log 20 / 784 ≈ 3.8×10−3. Hence liminfL γL > 0.
Scope and constants
Even periodic rings, liminf of finite gaps. Not a statement about every infinite-volume ground state: Cor 6.1 covers subsequential limits of periodic ground states only. The headline "resolves the Haldane conjecture" overstates this.
Thin seeds, tiny constants. S(60, 105/4) > 7/8 by 6×10−4; tightest scalar margin 4.7×10−5. Certified gap 4.8×10−4 against a true gap of about 0.41: a factor of a thousand.
Where the work is. Seed 1 from exact Z6,8,10(21/2), 59,049 states in integer arithmetic, a D = 26 matrix-product state giving Z120 > 1, and a 5/2-power raise to β = 105/4. Seed 2 from Z4..12(49/4): defects 0.048 and 0.18 at (36, 49/4), six doublings to below 0.01 at (2304, 784).
